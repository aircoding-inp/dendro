Work in this order.

1. Read what came with this guide: `profile`, `digests`, `decisions`, `problems`, `results`, and whatever else is there. `unread_days` have work and no digest: read them with dendro_day if the span depends on them. A record marked `cut` is whole in dendro_log.
2. Plan before writing: each part, the one thing it says, the ids that show it. A part with no record behind it is dropped, not padded.
3. Write for `audience`, in the project's language and its own words. A library has releases and users, a paper has claims and experiments, a client job has requirements and handovers.
4. Check every number, name and date against the record it cites, and cut what does not hold.
5. Save it with dendro_output, each section with the ids it rests on, and show it to the person.

A commit subject can claim more than was done, and a `reply` is what an agent answered, not proof that it is so: look for the record behind them. What the records do not show is said to be unknown or left out, never filled in. Open problems are stated, not hidden. No praise and no plans nobody stated. If `earlier` holds a version of this, say what changed since.
